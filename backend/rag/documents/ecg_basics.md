# ECG — Clinical Interpretation Guide

**Document Type:** Curated Educational Reference
**Source:** Compiled from standard cardiology textbooks

---

## Normal ECG Parameters

A normal 12-lead ECG shows:
- **Heart rate**: 60–100 bpm (sinus rhythm)
- **PR interval**: 120–200 ms
- **QRS duration**: Less than 120 ms
- **QT interval**: Corrected QT (QTc) less than 440 ms in males, less than 460 ms in females
- **Axis**: Normal axis between -30° and +90°

## Sinus Rhythm

Normal sinus rhythm requires:
- Regular P waves before each QRS complex
- Upright P waves in leads I, II, aVF
- P wave rate between 60 and 100 bpm
- Constant PR interval

## Common Arrhythmias

### Atrial Fibrillation
- Irregularly irregular rhythm
- Absence of distinct P waves; replaced by fibrillatory waves
- Variable ventricular rate
- Risk factors: hypertension, heart failure, valvular disease, thyrotoxicosis

### Atrial Flutter
- Sawtooth flutter waves (best seen in leads II, III, aVF, V1)
- Typically 300 bpm atrial rate with 2:1, 3:1, or 4:1 block
- Often associated with underlying heart disease

### Ventricular Tachycardia
- Wide complex tachycardia (QRS > 120 ms)
- Rate typically 150–250 bpm
- May be monomorphic or polymorphic
- Can degenerate into ventricular fibrillation

## ST Segment Changes

### ST Elevation
- Acute myocardial infarction (STEMI): ST elevation in contiguous leads
- Pericarditis: Diffuse ST elevation with PR depression
- Left ventricular aneurysm: Persistent ST elevation

### ST Depression
- Myocardial ischemia
- Digoxin effect (scooped ST depression)
- Left ventricular hypertrophy with strain pattern

## Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH)

Voltage criteria for LVH include:
- Sokolow-Lyon: S in V1 + R in V5 or V6 greater than 35 mm
- Cornell: R in aVL + S in V3 greater than 28 mm (males) or 20 mm (females)

LVH is commonly seen in:
- Chronic hypertension
- Aortic stenosis
- Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy

## Clinical Significance in Metabolic Disease

Patients with diabetes and hypertension are at increased risk for:
- Left ventricular hypertrophy due to chronic pressure overload
- Silent myocardial ischemia (ST changes without typical chest pain)
- QT prolongation from electrolyte disturbances or medications
- Atrial fibrillation from structural heart changes
- Diabetic autonomic neuropathy affecting heart rate variability

Regular ECG monitoring is recommended in these populations to detect early cardiac changes.

# Chest X-Ray — Clinical Interpretation Guide

**Document Type:** Curated Educational Reference
**Source:** Compiled from standard radiology textbooks

---

## Systematic Approach to Chest X-Ray Reading

A systematic approach ensures no findings are missed. The commonly used approach reviews:

1. **Technical quality**: Rotation, penetration, inspiration
2. **Airway**: Trachea midline, carina, bronchi
3. **Bones**: Ribs, clavicles, spine
4. **Cardiac**: Heart size, silhouette, mediastinum
5. **Diaphragm**: Costophrenic angles, free air
6. **Everything else**: Lungs, pleura, soft tissues

## Cardiothoracic Ratio

The cardiothoracic ratio (CTR) is the ratio of the maximum cardiac diameter to the maximum thoracic diameter on a PA (posteroanterior) film. A CTR greater than 0.5 suggests cardiomegaly. Common causes include:
- Congestive heart failure
- Valvular heart disease
- Dilated cardiomyopathy
- Pericardial effusion

## Lung Opacities

### Consolidation
Airspace opacity where air in the alveoli is replaced by fluid, pus, blood, or cells. Features include:
- Air bronchograms (air-filled bronchi visible within opacified lung)
- Lobar or segmental distribution
- Common causes: pneumonia, pulmonary edema, hemorrhage

### Ground-Glass Opacity
Hazy increased opacity that does not obscure underlying structures. Can be seen in:
- Early pneumonia
- Pulmonary edema
- Interstitial lung disease
- COVID-19 pneumonia

### Reticular Pattern
Network of lines suggesting interstitial disease. Causes include:
- Pulmonary fibrosis
- Lymphangitic carcinomatosis
- Chronic heart failure

## Pleural Effusion

Fluid in the pleural space appears as blunting of the costophrenic angle. On an upright film:
- Small effusions: meniscus sign at the costophrenic angle
- Moderate effusions: opacification of the lower hemithorax
- Large effusions: near-complete opacification with mediastinal shift

## Common Pathological Patterns

### Pneumonia
- Lobar consolidation with air bronchograms
- May see pleural effusion (parapneumonic effusion)
- Round pneumonia more common in children

### Pulmonary Edema
- Bilateral perihilar opacities (butterfly or bat-wing pattern)
- Kerley B lines (horizontal lines at lung bases)
- Upper lobe pulmonary venous distension (cephalization)
- Pleural effusions, often bilateral

### Pneumothorax
- Visible visceral pleural line with absent lung markings peripherally
- May cause mediastinal shift (tension pneumothorax — medical emergency)

## Clinical Significance

Chest X-ray remains the most commonly performed radiological examination. Correlation with clinical findings (symptoms, physical examination, laboratory results) is essential for accurate interpretation. AI-assisted analysis can help identify findings but clinical judgment is required for definitive interpretation.

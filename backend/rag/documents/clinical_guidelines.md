# General Clinical Guidelines — Decision Support Reference

**Document Type:** Curated Educational Reference
**Source:** Compiled from standard clinical practice guidelines

## Hypertension Management

### Blood Pressure Targets
- General population: Less than 140/90 mmHg
- Diabetic patients: Less than 130/80 mmHg (per ACC/AHA guidelines)
- CKD patients: Less than 130/80 mmHg
- Elderly (over 65 years): Less than 140/90 mmHg (less aggressive targets may be appropriate)

### First-Line Antihypertensives
- ACE inhibitors (e.g., Ramipril, Enalapril) — preferred in diabetic patients with proteinuria
- ARBs (e.g., Losartan, Telmisartan) — alternative to ACE inhibitors
- Calcium channel blockers (e.g., Amlodipine)
- Thiazide diuretics (e.g., Hydrochlorothiazide)

Combination therapy is often required. Most patients need two or more agents to reach target.

## Type 2 Diabetes Management

### Glycemic Targets
- HbA1c less than 7.0% for most adults
- HbA1c less than 8.0% for elderly or patients with significant comorbidities
- Individualized targets based on patient factors

### Stepwise Approach
1. Lifestyle modification (diet, exercise, weight management)
2. Metformin as first-line pharmacotherapy
3. Second agent based on patient profile (SGLT2 inhibitors preferred for CKD or heart failure, GLP-1 receptor agonists for cardiovascular risk reduction)
4. Insulin therapy when oral agents insufficient

### Monitoring
- HbA1c every 3–6 months
- Fasting glucose and postprandial glucose monitoring
- Annual screening: kidney function (eGFR, urine albumin), eye examination, foot examination
- Regular cardiovascular risk assessment

## Chronic Kidney Disease

### Classification (Based on eGFR)
- Stage 1: eGFR 90 or above, kidney damage present
- Stage 2: eGFR 60–89, mild decrease
- Stage 3a: eGFR 45–59, mild-moderate decrease
- Stage 3b: eGFR 30–44, moderate-severe decrease
- Stage 4: eGFR 15–29, severe decrease
- Stage 5: eGFR less than 15, kidney failure

### Management Principles
- Treat underlying cause (diabetes control, blood pressure control)
- ACE inhibitors or ARBs to reduce proteinuria
- SGLT2 inhibitors for eGFR 20–60 with albuminuria
- Avoid nephrotoxic drugs
- Monitor potassium levels closely
- Refer to nephrology when eGFR less than 30 or rapid decline

## Cardiovascular Risk Assessment

### Risk Factors
- Hypertension
- Diabetes mellitus
- Dyslipidemia (high LDL, low HDL, high triglycerides)
- Smoking
- Family history of premature cardiovascular disease
- Obesity
- Physical inactivity
- Chronic kidney disease

### Lipid Management
- Statin therapy for all diabetic patients aged 40–75 years
- High-intensity statins for patients with established cardiovascular disease
- Target LDL less than 70 mg/dL in very high-risk patients
- Add ezetimibe if target not achieved with statin alone

## Cognitive Impairment Assessment

### Screening
- Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE)
- Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA)
- Clock Drawing Test

### Common Causes
- Alzheimer's disease (most common neurodegenerative cause)
- Vascular cognitive impairment (associated with cerebrovascular disease)
- Mixed dementia (Alzheimer's + vascular)
- Reversible causes: B12 deficiency, thyroid disease, normal pressure hydrocephalus, depression

### Vascular Cognitive Impairment
- Associated with cerebral small vessel disease on MRI
- Risk factors: hypertension, diabetes, smoking
- Management focuses on vascular risk factor control
- Cholinesterase inhibitors (Donepezil) may be considered
- No disease-modifying therapy currently available

## Integrated Care for Complex Patients

Patients with multiple comorbidities require:
- Coordinated care across specialties
- Regular medication review to avoid polypharmacy complications
- Attention to drug interactions (e.g., ACE inhibitors + potassium-sparing diuretics)
- Patient education on self-management
- Regular follow-up and monitoring schedules
- Assessment for depression and cognitive decline in chronic disease patients

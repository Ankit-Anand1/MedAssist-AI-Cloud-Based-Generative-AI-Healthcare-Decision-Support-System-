# Common Blood Test Parameters — Clinical Reference

**Document Type:** Curated Educational Reference
**Source:** Compiled from standard clinical pathology references

---

## Complete Blood Count (CBC)

### Hemoglobin (Hb)
- **Normal**: Males 13.5–17.5 g/dL; Females 12.0–16.0 g/dL
- **Low (Anemia)**: Iron deficiency, chronic disease, vitamin B12/folate deficiency, bone marrow disorders
- **High (Polycythemia)**: Dehydration, polycythemia vera, chronic hypoxia, high altitude

### White Blood Cell Count (WBC)
- **Normal**: 4,500–11,000 cells/μL
- **High (Leukocytosis)**: Infection, inflammation, stress, leukemia, corticosteroid use
- **Low (Leukopenia)**: Bone marrow suppression, viral infections, autoimmune disorders

### Platelet Count
- **Normal**: 150,000–400,000/μL
- **Low (Thrombocytopenia)**: ITP, DIC, liver disease, drug-induced, bone marrow failure
- **High (Thrombocytosis)**: Reactive (infection, inflammation), myeloproliferative disorders

## Metabolic Panel

### Blood Glucose
- **Fasting glucose**: 70–100 mg/dL (normal), 100–125 mg/dL (prediabetes), 126 mg/dL or above (diabetes)
- **Random glucose**: Less than 200 mg/dL (normal)
- **HbA1c**: Less than 5.7% (normal), 5.7–6.4% (prediabetes), 6.5% or above (diabetes)

HbA1c reflects average blood glucose over 2–3 months. Target for diabetic patients is generally less than 7.0%, though individualized targets may apply based on age, comorbidities, and hypoglycemia risk.

### Renal Function
- **Creatinine**: Males 0.7–1.3 mg/dL; Females 0.6–1.1 mg/dL
- **BUN (Blood Urea Nitrogen)**: 7–20 mg/dL
- **eGFR (Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate)**:
  - Greater than 90 mL/min: Normal
  - 60–89 mL/min: Stage 2 CKD (mildly decreased)
  - 45–59 mL/min: Stage 3a CKD (mildly to moderately decreased)
  - 30–44 mL/min: Stage 3b CKD (moderately to severely decreased)
  - 15–29 mL/min: Stage 4 CKD (severely decreased)
  - Less than 15 mL/min: Stage 5 CKD (kidney failure)

### Liver Function
- **ALT (Alanine Aminotransferase)**: 7–56 U/L
- **AST (Aspartate Aminotransferase)**: 10–40 U/L
- **ALP (Alkaline Phosphatase)**: 44–147 U/L
- **Total Bilirubin**: 0.1–1.2 mg/dL
- **Albumin**: 3.5–5.5 g/dL
- Elevated ALT/AST may indicate hepatocellular injury. AST:ALT ratio greater than 2 suggests alcoholic liver disease.

## Lipid Profile

- **Total Cholesterol**: Less than 200 mg/dL (desirable)
- **LDL Cholesterol**: Less than 100 mg/dL (optimal); less than 70 mg/dL in high-risk cardiovascular patients
- **HDL Cholesterol**: Greater than 40 mg/dL (males), greater than 50 mg/dL (females)
- **Triglycerides**: Less than 150 mg/dL (normal)

Low HDL and high LDL increase cardiovascular risk. In diabetic patients, aggressive lipid management is recommended with statin therapy.

## Thyroid Function

- **TSH**: 0.4–4.0 mU/L
- **Free T4**: 0.8–1.8 ng/dL
- **Free T3**: 2.3–4.2 pg/mL
- Elevated TSH with low T4 indicates hypothyroidism
- Low TSH with high T4 indicates hyperthyroidism

## Electrolytes

- **Sodium**: 136–145 mEq/L
- **Potassium**: 3.5–5.0 mEq/L
- **Chloride**: 98–106 mEq/L
- **Calcium**: 8.5–10.5 mg/dL
- **Magnesium**: 1.7–2.2 mg/dL

Potassium imbalances can cause cardiac arrhythmias. Hyperkalemia is common in chronic kidney disease and with ACE inhibitors or ARBs.

## Clinical Significance in Comorbid Patients

Patients with multiple conditions (diabetes, hypertension, kidney disease) require integrated interpretation of blood results:
- Declining eGFR in a diabetic patient suggests diabetic nephropathy
- HbA1c trending upward despite medication may indicate poor compliance or need for therapy adjustment
- Low HDL combined with high triglycerides is characteristic of diabetic dyslipidemia
- Hyperkalemia in CKD patients on ACE inhibitors needs close monitoring
- Anemia of chronic disease is common in patients with CKD and diabetes
